# FullHouse: a short guide

For owners, admins, staff and bookkeepers. The same steps are in the app under **Help**.

## Getting around

The sidebar starts with Today and Recommendations, then one section for each part of the business: **Inventory**
(Inventory, Suppliers), **Staffing** (Schedule & staff), **Customers** (Customer requests), **Invoices & prices** in
the order an invoice moves through it (Invoices, Questions, Items, Digest, Conversation), and **Menu**. Then **Agent**
(Assistant Manager, Agent activity), **Records** (Profit & forecast, History & undo) and **Restaurant** (Profile,
Help). On a phone, Today, Inventory, Schedule and Recommendations are the bottom bar, and everything else is behind
**More**.

- **Today**: low stock, recommendations waiting, and the day's figures.
- **Inventory**: stock on hand, deliveries, counts, each item's history, and the last price the invoices show for it.
- **Schedule & staff**: the team and the published schedule.
- **Recommendations**: changes the Assistant Manager proposed, waiting for a decision.
- **Customer requests**: what customers told you, in their own words.

Invoices & prices, start to finish:

- **Invoices**: where they arrive. Add one (a photo or PDF, Bill.com, pasted text, or by hand), see what was read
  from it, and confirm any that needed a check before it counts.
- **Questions**: the one human step. The agent asks about invoice lines it couldn't place: is this the same item, how
  many come in a pack. Tap an answer; the card goes, and the answer is kept for next time.
- **Items**: the price history. Everything you buy, with the last price paid and how it moved over eight weeks, by
  supplier. Open one for the chart, the invoice lines behind it, and "Draft message to supplier".
- **Digest**: what the weekly check found: what changed in your supplier prices, what it cost, and what to do. Three
  lists, always in the same order: Switch supplier, Price went up, Owed to you.
- **Conversation**: where the loop closes. Everything said between you and the agent: its questions and your answers, your questions
  and its answers, and the drafts to suppliers, each with the invoices it used.
The field that says **Ask about prices, invoices, or suppliers** is on every price page: at the bottom on a phone,
top right on a desktop. It is the way to talk to the agent about prices. On the other pages, **New task** gives the
Assistant Manager something to do.

## Prices, week by week

**Add an invoice** (owners and admins). Invoices → **Add invoice**. Snap a photo or upload a PDF and the lines are
read for you, each one marked if it was hard to make out (a site with no model connected reads PDFs but not photos,
and says so; type the lines from the photo instead); pull bills from Bill.com if the site is connected; paste the
text of a forwarded invoice; or type the lines. Check them, add the printed total if you have it, and save. If the
lines don't add to the total or something was hard to read, the invoice is kept but marked **Not counted until
checked**; press **Looks right** once you've compared it with the paper and it counts from then on.

**Answer the agent's questions.** Questions shows a count and the cards. Each has the invoice line, one question and
two answers; **Something else** lets you type the item's name or the count per case. Anyone on the team can answer.
The agent asks at most five a week; the rest wait their turn, biggest dollars first.

**Get the digest by mail.** It arrives Monday morning, to owners and admins, with the same three sections and a
"Not useful" link on every card. **Email me this digest** on the Digest page sends it now.

**Read the digest.** The number at the top is the dollars at stake this week. Each card says what you paid, where,
and what changed, and has one button: **Move <item>** records that you're switching (nothing is ordered), **Draft
message** writes to the rep. **Not useful** hides the item for four weeks; it's the only feedback there is.

**Draft a message to a supplier** (owners and admins). From a card or an item page. The draft is three sentences and
the invoice lines it rests on. Edit it, then **Send via email** (a confirm sheet shows who and what; your own mail app
opens with it filled in), **Copy**, or **Discard**. The rep's name and email are remembered for next time.

**Ask.** Type a question anywhere: "what did I pay for tomatoes last month", "which supplier is cheapest on paper
goods", "how much did Sysco short me this quarter", "talking points for tomorrow's meeting". Answers are two sentences
with the numbers and chips for the invoices used.

**Fix a pack size.** On an item page the line "Reading this as 24 ct per case" has a **Wrong?** link. Enter the right
count; every line of that item is re-read.

**Stock from the invoices.** An item on the invoices is counted as the Inventory item its name matches ("Tomatoes,
25 lb case" as Tomatoes). The item page says which; **Change** picks another, or none. From then on a line on a
confirmed invoice is booked into Inventory as a delivery, once, in the agent's name, with the invoice in the note, and
the Inventory page shows the last price paid for each item with a link to its prices. When an item is at or below its
reorder point and the invoices price it, a reorder is proposed under Recommendations from the cheapest supplier of
the last four weeks (its own supplier for beer, wine and liquor), in whole packs, with what it comes to. Approve it
and the order is placed (see "Track an order" below); a delivery that lifts the stock, or an order already on its way,
withdraws it. Nothing is sent to a supplier for you. An item counted in a unit the invoice can't be read in (bunches
against pounds) is priced but never booked, and the page says so.

**Bookkeepers.** An owner invites an accountant from Profile → Team access with the role *Bookkeeper*. They sign in
to a table of clients (questions waiting, last digest, dollars flagged this month) and can answer any client's
questions. Owners see those questions as resolved, not as answered by the bookkeeper.

## Connections and prime cost

Switched on under **Profile → Features** by the owner. **Connections** then appears under Restaurant.

**Point of sale and payroll.** Connect Square or Gusto with their buttons (the owner signs in there once), or
drop a CSV export in: sales by day or by item from Toast, Square or Clover, or payroll hours and dollars by day.
The columns are matched from the headers and shown back before anything is imported; correct a column and the
mapping is remembered for next time. A file with a bad row is imported whole or not at all, and says which line.

**Invoices by email.** Each restaurant has its own address on the Connections page, with a copy button. Add the
senders allowed to use it (an address, or `@supplier.com` for everyone there); mail from anyone else is kept but not
read, and the page says who it was from. Attachments are read by the same reader as an upload and land under
Invoices, checked the same way.

**Prime cost.** Once a week has sales and labor for every day, the Monday digest opens with one line: prime cost as
a share of net sales, with food and labor beside it. Food is the week's invoice lines (food, dry goods and
beverages), paper is paper, labor is what payroll says. A week missing any day's sales or labor shows what to
connect instead of a wrong number.

**Accounting.** With QuickBooks connected, a checked invoice can be pushed as a bill by a manager or bookkeeper,
once; nothing is pulled and nothing is paid.

## Documents, agreed prices and recipes

Switched on under **Profile → Features**. **Documents** appears under Invoices & prices, **Recipes** under Menu.

**Documents.** Upload anything, or forward it to your inbound address: it is read for what it is. An invoice goes to
Invoices as an upload would. A supplier's price list becomes **agreed price** proposals, one per line that matches an
item you already buy. A recipe card becomes a **recipe** proposal. A license, permit, inspection report or warranty is
read with its dates for the calendar. When the reader isn't sure what a file is, it waits under "Needs a look" and
you say which it is.

**Agreed prices.** A price you agreed with a supplier, from their price list or quote, or set on an item's page with
**Set agreed price**. Each is a proposal until approved. From then on any invoice line billed above it by more than 1%
or two cents shows under **Owed to you** in the digest, with the dollars over and both the invoice and the price list
it rests on. Nothing is ever compared with a "market" price; only with your own agreements.

**Recipes and plate cost.** Once sales are connected, the agent proposes a recipe for each top-selling dish, built
from the dish's name and what you actually buy; ingredients it can't find on your invoices are a question on the
proposal, never a guess. Approve it (edit quantities first if you like) and the dish gets a plate cost each week from
the latest price your most-used supplier charged for each ingredient, with the invoice lines behind it on the recipe's
page. Editing a recipe is itself a proposal.

**Menu margin.** A dish whose plate cost rose 5% or more in a week, or whose margin fell below your median, gets a
card under **Menu margin** in the digest with the weekly dollars. **Reprice or reduce portion** suggests a new price
as a proposal, with the plate cost before and after. You change the price in your POS; FullHouse never writes to a
menu.

## Order drafts, forecast and prep sheets

Switched on under **Profile → Features** (`Order drafts, forecasting, prep sheets`), with each schedule under
**Schedules** on the same tab.

**Order drafts.** On Sunday evening (or on request) the agent drafts this week's order to each supplier you have
bought from at least four times in eight weeks: for each item, the median weekly quantity over those eight weeks at
the latest price, with the invoice lines it came from. Each is a proposal under Recommendations; approving it makes
a draft under On order, which you send to the rep or mark placed. Three drafts a week at most.

**Forecast.** Nightly, for each dish with at least four weeks of sales: the expected quantity for each of the next
seven days, from the same weekday over the last six weeks (recent weeks weigh more) and the trend of the last two
weeks against the four before, with a low and high. When the day's sales arrive, the miss is recorded, and the
digest ends with "Forecast accuracy last week". No weather or holidays: a plainer model, on purpose.

**Prep sheets.** Daily (or on request), for the next two days: the forecast times each recipe's lines, per
ingredient, as a proposal. Approve it and it is a printable page under Menu → Prep sheets. Nothing is subtracted for
stock on hand, because nothing here counts stock.

**Recovered to date.** The digest also ends with the dollars a week of every card acted on: an item you moved, a
price rise or a credit you wrote to the rep about.

## Everyday jobs

**Log a delivery.** Inventory → find the item → **Record change** → **Delivery received** → enter the amount →
**Save change**. For items bought by the case, switch to cases and enter the number of cases. If the connection
drops, press Save again: it won't be counted twice.

**Record what was used or wasted.** Same place, choose **Used** or **Wasted**.

**Count stock.** **Record change** → **Stock count** → enter what's really there. The difference is kept in the
item's history.

**Add an item.** Inventory → **Add item**. Set how it's counted (each, lb, L…), its reorder point, and, if you buy
it by the case or box, how many come in one.

**Bring in your stock list** (owners and admins). Save your spreadsheet as CSV → Inventory → **Import**. Check the
preview: problems are listed by line. Nothing is saved until every line is right.

**The schedule.** Owners and admins add shifts as drafts, then **Publish**. Staff see only published shifts. A shift
that breaks the restaurant's rules (too long, too little rest, too many hours in the week) is refused with the
reason.

**Recommendations** (owners and admins). Each says what it would change and why. **Approve** carries it out;
**Reject** drops it. Approving a reorder places the order; the stock moves when it arrives. To ask the Assistant
Manager something, press **New task**, e.g. "Check stock and reorder anything low".

**Track an order.** Inventory → **On order** lists every order drafted, placed and not yet here: the amount, the
supplier, when it was ordered and when it's expected (the supplier's lead time unless you say otherwise). Approving
a reorder **drafts** the order; it is not an order the supplier knows about until you **Send to rep** (a mail with
the lines and a PDF, to the rep remembered on a draft message or an address you give) or mark it **Placed by phone**.
Open the order's own page from its name to print it. An order placed outside the app is added with **Record an
order** (owners and admins). **Mark shipped** when the supplier says it's on its way. One past its expected date
shows as overdue, here and on Today. **Receive** when it arrives: enter what actually came (all of it by default)
and a note, and it is booked into stock, once, under "Order received" in the history. If the invoice for the order
is added first, the invoice receives it line by line, and an order received by hand is matched to its invoice when
that comes, so nothing is counted twice. An invoice that brings less than was ordered shows under **Owed to you** in
the digest as a short delivery. **Cancel** drops an order that was never placed or never came (owners and admins).

**Undo a mistake** (owners and admins). History & undo → find the change → **Undo**. If something has changed
since, you'll be asked before it's undone anyway.

**Invite someone** (owners and admins). Profile → Team access → make an invite link and send it. It works once and
expires after a week.

## When something's wrong

- **Offline**: a red bar says so. You can look around; saving needs a connection.
- **The AI is unavailable**: everything else still works (stock, the schedule, the menu, history).
- **Signed out unexpectedly**: sign in again. Anything already saved is kept.
- **Something looks wrong, or you're stuck**: press **Send feedback** (in the sidebar, or on the Help page) and say
  what happened. It goes to the people running FullHouse, with the page you were on.

## Your data

Owners and admins can download everything the restaurant keeps in FullHouse, any time: Help → **Download all data**.
It's a zip of spreadsheet files, one per kind of record.
